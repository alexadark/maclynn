Choosing a note-taker

How we'll capture sales meetings, in person and online, and why we recommend Granola. Prepared by Alexandra Spalato for Rachel, Alexa and Tori.

Every proposal starts with a conversation. Today, most of that conversation lives in Tori's notebook. She photographs the pages, types them up with ChatGPT, and then writes the proposal. It works, but it eats evenings, and some meetings never get written up at all.

The new sales workflow changes the first step. With the client's permission, the meeting is transcribed. Claude turns the transcript into notes in Tori's own format, files them in GoHighLevel, and drafts the proposal. Tori still writes her personal impressions by hand. The machine takes the dictation, she keeps the judgement.

For that to work, we need one tool that's easy to use, invisible to the client, and hands everything to Claude. This page explains which one, and why.

What the tool has to do

  • Work from the phone. Tori meets most leads face to face, in cafés, hotels and offices. The tool has to work with the phone lying on the table.
  • Work on WhatsApp calls, which is how many clients prefer to talk.
  • Work on Zoom and Teams without a bot. Nothing should join the call or appear in the conversation. A robot attendee feels wrong in a first meeting with a private client.
  • Talk to Claude and ChatGPT. The transcript has to reach the AI without copy and paste, whichever one the team is using that month.
  • Keep client data out of AI training, with a standard data processing agreement in its terms.
  • Be easy for Tori. One tap to start, nothing to configure in the meeting.

Our recommendation: Granola

Granola, Business plan. $14 per person per month. No device to buy.

Website: granola.ai · pricing · security

Granola is a London company, and its notes are widely seen as the best on the market. It ticks every box.

  • On the phone: the iPhone app records face-to-face meetings. Tori opens the app, taps record and puts the phone on the table. It tells up to ten voices apart.
  • Phone calls: calls made from inside the Granola app on iPhone are transcribed too.
  • Zoom, Teams and Meet: the Mac or Windows app listens to the computer's own sound. No bot joins, and nothing appears in the call. For Teams, use the desktop app rather than the browser.
  • WhatsApp: calls taken in the WhatsApp app on the computer are captured the same way, with nothing visible to the client.
  • Claude and ChatGPT: Granola connects to both. Claude can read a meeting as soon as it ends.
  • GoHighLevel: Zapier and an API let us send notes to the contact automatically.
  • Privacy: the audio is deleted once it's transcribed. A data processing agreement comes with every plan. SOC 2 Type II certified.
  • Team: shared folders and a team dictionary, so names like SmartMatch come out right for everyone.

One setting per person

On the Business plan, each person switches off AI training in their own settings, and sets how long transcripts are kept. It takes a minute, once. Only the Enterprise plan enforces it for the whole team, and that isn't worth $35 a seat for a team our size.

WhatsApp calls on the phone

This is the one gap, and it isn't Granola's fault. The iPhone doesn't let any app listen to another app's call. No note-taker app can transcribe a WhatsApp call taken on the phone itself.

There are two ways round it.

  • Take the call on the computer. WhatsApp has a desktop app for Mac and Windows. Calls made there are captured by Granola like any Zoom call. This covers most planned calls, and it's free.
  • Clip a recorder to the phone. The Plaud Note Pro (about £169) is a card that sticks magnetically to the back of the iPhone. It picks up the call through the phone's vibrations, so it records WhatsApp, regular calls and FaceTime alike. It also works on the table for face-to-face meetings. Only worth it for someone who takes many WhatsApp calls on the move.

If most of the team's client calls happen on WhatsApp away from a desk, the better choice is to put everyone on Plaud instead: the recorder for calls and meetings, and its desktop app for Zoom and Teams, again without a bot. One system, at the cost of a device per person and slightly less polished notes.

Why not the others

  • Wispr Flow is the closest call. Rachel already uses it for dictation, and in August it added a meeting notetaker that works without a bot and connects to Claude. But the notetaker only runs on a Mac or Windows computer, not on the phone, so it can't help Tori in a café. Keep Wispr Flow for dictation. It and Granola do different jobs.
  • Plaud is the only option that records WhatsApp calls on the phone. If that's how most client calls happen, it becomes the pick (see above). Otherwise it's a device to carry and charge for every salesperson.
  • Fireflies is already paid for, on five seats. But it joins calls as a bot, and its transcripts never became part of the routine. We'd cancel it once Granola is running.
  • Fathom is built for video calls and joins them as a bot.
  • Teams Copilot only works inside Teams meetings. It can't help in a café.

Do we need a device?

Not for meetings. For two people at a table, an iPhone lying flat between them records clearly. It's discreet, everyone already has one, and everything stays in one app.

A device only earns its place for WhatsApp calls taken on the phone, as explained above.

Asking for consent

Recording a client is fine as long as they know and agree. Maclynn's conversations often touch on health and private life, so a little care goes a long way.

  • Ask at the start of every meeting, in plain words. Something like:

"Would you mind if I take notes with an AI assistant today? It's only so I can prepare an accurate proposal for you. It stays within Maclynn, and you can ask us to delete it at any time."

  • If the answer is no, Tori takes notes by hand as she does today. Nothing else changes.
  • Add a line to the privacy notice about meeting notes, and decide how long transcripts are kept.
