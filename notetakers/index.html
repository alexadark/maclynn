Choosing a note-taker

How we'll capture every client conversation, in person, on video and on WhatsApp, and hand it to Claude. Prepared by Alexandra Spalato for Rachel, Alexa and Tori.

Every proposal starts with a conversation. Today, most of that conversation lives in Tori's notebook. She photographs the pages, types them up with ChatGPT, and then writes the proposal. It works, but it eats evenings, and some meetings never get written up at all.

The new sales workflow changes the first step. With the client's permission, the conversation is transcribed. Claude turns the transcript into notes in Tori's own format, files them in GoHighLevel, and drafts the proposal. Tori still writes her personal impressions by hand. The machine takes the dictation, she keeps the judgement.

What we need

Maclynn's clients talk in three places, and the tool has to follow them into all three.

  • Face to face, in cafés, hotels and offices, with only a phone on the table.
  • On Zoom and Teams, with nothing joining the call or appearing in the conversation.
  • On WhatsApp, sometimes from the desk, often on the move.

Whatever records the conversation must hand the transcript to Claude or ChatGPT without copy and paste, and keep client data out of AI training.

Our recommendation: Granola, plus a Plaud card for calls on the move

Granola Business for the whole team, $14 per person per month. It covers face-to-face meetings, Zoom, Teams and WhatsApp on the computer.

A Plaud Note Pro for whoever takes WhatsApp calls on the phone, about £169 once. Its free plan includes 300 minutes of transcription a month.

No single app can do everything, because the iPhone doesn't let one app listen to another app's call. Granola is the best note-taker for everything else. The Plaud card fills the one gap it can't.

Granola: the everyday tool

granola.ai · pricing

  • Face to face: open the iPhone app, tap record, put the phone on the table. It tells up to ten voices apart.
  • Zoom, Teams and Meet: the Mac or Windows app listens to the computer's own sound. No bot joins, and nothing appears in the call. For Teams, use the desktop app rather than the browser.
  • WhatsApp on the computer: calls taken in WhatsApp for Mac or Windows are captured the same way.
  • Phone calls: calls dialled from inside the Granola app on iPhone are transcribed too.
  • Privacy: audio is deleted once transcribed. A data processing agreement comes with every plan. Each person switches off AI training once in their settings.

Plaud Note Pro: for WhatsApp on the move

uk.plaud.ai · WhatsApp calls

  • How it works: a card the size of a credit card that clips magnetically to the back of the iPhone. It picks up the call through the phone's vibrations, so it records WhatsApp, FaceTime and ordinary calls alike, both sides.
  • Automatic: it notices a call has started and switches mode by itself. Laid on a table, it records a meeting instead.
  • Discreet: it looks like a card holder, not equipment.
  • Free plan: 300 minutes a month come with the device, roughly ten half-hour calls. That's plenty if most calls still go through Granola.

Getting the transcripts into Claude and ChatGPT

Both tools have official connectors, listed in Claude's connector directory and in ChatGPT. Connect once, sign in, and the assistant can search every meeting and read the full transcript. No export, no copy and paste. Claude can look across Granola and Plaud in the same conversation, so two tools don't mean two places to search.

The alternative: Plaud for everything

If most client calls turn out to be WhatsApp calls on the move, it's simpler to put the whole team on Plaud. The card covers meetings and phone calls, and the free Plaud Desktop app covers Zoom and Teams, again with no bot. One system and one connector.

  • Cost: a card for each person, plus the Team plan at $20 per person per month on annual billing. That's a launch price until the end of November 2026, then $25.
  • Trade-off: a device to carry and charge, and notes that are a little less polished than Granola's. Since Claude rewrites the notes in Tori's format anyway, that matters less than it sounds.

We'd start with the combination above and switch only if the Plaud card ends up doing most of the work.

Why not the others

  • Wispr Flow is great for dictation, and Rachel already uses it. Its new meeting notetaker works without a bot and connects to Claude, but it only runs on a computer, not on the phone. Keep it for dictation.
  • Fireflies is already paid for, on five seats. It joins calls as a bot, and its transcripts never became part of the routine. We'd cancel it once the new setup runs.
  • Fathom is built for video calls and joins them as a bot.
  • Teams Copilot only works inside Teams meetings. It can't help in a café or on WhatsApp.

Asking for consent

Recording a client is fine as long as they know and agree. Maclynn's conversations often touch on health and private life, so a little care goes a long way.

  • Ask at the start of every meeting or call, in plain words. Something like:

"Would you mind if I take notes with an AI assistant today? It's only so I can prepare an accurate proposal for you. It stays within Maclynn, and you can ask us to delete it at any time."

  • If the answer is no, Tori takes notes by hand as she does today. Nothing else changes.
  • Add a line to the privacy notice about meeting notes, and decide how long transcripts are kept.
