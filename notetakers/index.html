Choosing a note-taker

Two good options to capture client conversations, in person, on video and on WhatsApp, and hand them to Claude. Features and prices side by side. Prepared by Alexandra Spalato for Rachel, Alexa and Tori.

Every proposal starts with a conversation. Today, most of that conversation lives in Tori's notebook. She photographs the pages, types them up with ChatGPT, and then writes the proposal. It works, but it eats evenings, and some meetings never get written up at all.

The new sales workflow changes the first step. With the client's permission, the conversation is transcribed. Claude turns the transcript into notes in Tori's own format, files them in GoHighLevel, and drafts the proposal. Tori still writes her personal impressions by hand. The machine takes the dictation, she keeps the judgement.

What we need

Maclynn's clients talk in three places, and the tool has to follow them.

  • Face to face, in cafés, hotels and offices.
  • On Zoom and Teams, with nothing joining the call or appearing in the conversation.
  • On WhatsApp, from the desk or on the move.

The transcript then has to reach Claude or ChatGPT without copy and paste, and client data has to stay out of AI training.

Two tools do this well: Granola, an app, and Plaud, a small recorder with its own app. Both connect directly to Claude and ChatGPT, so either one plugs into the sales workflow the same way. We only need one of them.

The two options

Granola

An app on the phone and the computer. $14 per person per month.
  • Nothing to buy or carry.
  • The best notes on the market.
  • Covers in-person meetings, video calls and WhatsApp on the computer.
  • Can't record a WhatsApp call taken on the phone.

granola.ai · pricing

Plaud

A card-sized recorder, about £169 each, plus an app. Free plan, or $20 per person per month for the team plan.
  • Records everything, including WhatsApp calls on the phone.
  • Clips to the back of the iPhone and switches mode by itself.
  • A device per person to carry and charge.
  • Notes a little less polished than Granola's.

uk.plaud.ai · plans

How to choose

  • Choose Granola if client WhatsApp calls can be taken at the desk, in WhatsApp for Mac or Windows. It's cheaper, there's nothing to carry, and the notes are the best.
  • Choose Plaud if many client calls happen on WhatsApp on the phone, away from a desk. It's the only way to capture them, because the iPhone doesn't let an app listen to another app's call. Plaud gets round this by picking up the call through the phone's vibrations.

Getting the transcripts into Claude and ChatGPT

Both tools are listed in Claude's connector directory and in ChatGPT. Connect once, sign in, and the assistant can search every conversation and read the full transcript. No export, no copy and paste.

In our workflow, Tori asks Claude for her notes right after the meeting. Claude reads the transcript through the connector, writes the notes in her format and files them in GoHighLevel. If we later want this to happen with nobody asking, Zapier can pass each new transcript along automatically, with either tool.

Why not the others

  • Wispr Flow is great for dictation, and Rachel already uses it. Its new meeting notetaker works without a bot and connects to Claude, but it only runs on a computer, not on the phone. Keep it for dictation.
  • Fireflies is already paid for, on five seats. It joins calls as a bot, and its transcripts never became part of the routine. We'd cancel it once the new tool runs.
  • Fathom is built for video calls and joins them as a bot.
  • Teams Copilot only works inside Teams meetings. It can't help in a café or on WhatsApp.

Asking for consent

Recording a client is fine as long as they know and agree. Maclynn's conversations often touch on health and private life, so a little care goes a long way.

  • Ask at the start of every meeting or call, in plain words. Something like:

"Would you mind if I take notes with an AI assistant today? It's only so I can prepare an accurate proposal for you. It stays within Maclynn, and you can ask us to delete it at any time."

  • If the answer is no, Tori takes notes by hand as she does today. Nothing else changes.
  • Add a line to the privacy notice about meeting notes, and decide how long transcripts are kept.

Where it fits in the sales workflow

Conversation, with consent→ Granola or Plaud transcribes→ Claude writes the notes→ Notes on the GoHighLevel contact→ Draft proposal

From there, the proposal and the Private Membership Agreement follow the new sales workflow, which replaces PandaDoc before 8 November.

Next step

One question decides it: how often does Tori take client calls on WhatsApp on her phone, away from a desk? If rarely, we start Granola on its free plan this week. If often, we order a Plaud Note Pro for her and try it on her next calls. Either way, I connect it to Claude and check her notes come out right before the team signs up.
